Part II - Socioeconomic Inequality in Education across the Arab World¶

by Malak Battatt¶

Investigation Overview¶

This presentation explores how socioeconomic status shapes math performance across six Arab countries that participated in PISA 2022: Palestine, Jordan, Morocco, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE.

The key insights I want to communicate are:

  1. Math scores vary considerably across Arab countries, with UAE and Qatar scoring significantly higher than Jordan, Morocco, and Palestine.
  2. A student's socioeconomic background is strongly associated with their math score — the gap between the poorest and richest students is about 95 points across the region.
  3. Qatar and UAE show the largest within-country inequality, while Jordan and Palestine have the smallest gaps.
  4. Girls outperform boys in math in 5 out of 6 countries, despite reporting higher math anxiety.

Dataset Overview¶

The data comes from the PISA 2022 Student Questionnaire (OECD). After filtering to Arab-world participating countries and selecting relevant variables, the dataset contains 61,775 students across Palestine, Jordan, Morocco, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE. The main outcome variable is math_score, computed as the average of 10 plausible values provided by PISA.

How do math scores compare across Arab countries?¶

The first thing I wanted to understand is how much math performance varies across the six countries. The chart below shows the distribution of math scores for each country, ordered from lowest to highest mean score.

All six countries fall below the OECD average of 472. UAE (434) and Qatar (412) are the closest to it, while Jordan (360), Morocco (363), and Palestine (365) are furthest away. Saudi Arabia sits in between at 389. UAE and Qatar also have much wider boxes, meaning more variation in scores within those countries.

How does socioeconomic status relate to math performance?¶

The ESCS index measures a student's socioeconomic, cultural, and economic background. Here I look at how math scores change across SES quartiles within each country — this directly addresses the inequality question.

Every country shows a consistent increase from Q1 to Q4. Qatar and UAE have the largest gaps between their lowest and highest quartiles (around 95–100 points), while Jordan and Palestine have the smallest (around 43–50 points). Across all countries, Q1 students score below the regional mean of 400, while Q4 students score above it — except in Jordan and Palestine where even Q4 barely reaches 400.

Does the gender gap in math scores hold across SES levels?¶

From the exploratory analysis, girls outperform boys in 5 out of 6 countries. Here I look at whether this advantage holds across all SES levels, or whether it changes depending on family background.

In Jordan, Qatar, and Palestine, the female line is steeper than the male line — girls gain more from higher SES in those countries. In Morocco and UAE the lines are close together. Saudi Arabia is the only country where the male line is steeper and boys score slightly higher overall. The gender advantage for girls is not uniform — it varies by both country and SES level.

Do girls report more math anxiety than boys, even though they score higher?¶

One pattern that stood out in the exploration was that girls report higher math anxiety than boys in most countries, yet still score higher in math. The next two charts show this — first the overall anxiety distribution by gender, then how it breaks down per country.

The math anxiety index is a standardized WLE scale centered at 0 — negative values mean below-average anxiety, positive values mean above-average. Both distributions follow a similar multimodal shape with three visible peaks. The female curve is shifted slightly to the right, meaning girls report marginally higher anxiety on average (mean: 0.26 vs 0.10 for boys).

Math anxiety by country and gender¶

Breaking the anxiety picture down by country shows that the gender difference isn't the same everywhere.

Girls report higher mean anxiety than boys in 5 out of 6 countries. The largest gaps are in Morocco (F: 0.48 vs M: 0.34), Qatar (F: 0.26 vs M: 0.09), and UAE (F: 0.27 vs M: 0.00). Jordan is the only country where boys report slightly higher anxiety than girls. Despite this, girls still score higher in math in all those same countries.
